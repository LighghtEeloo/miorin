# Miorin

A cross-platform application that continuously collects raw material from the user's clipboard and watched folders, and lets them drag, edit, and compose that material into structured notes and daily diaries, with optional LLM assistance for summarization and prioritization.

## Data and Privacy Statement

All data is stored locally on your machine, and you are in full control of your data. The project is open source and free to use, and everyone is welcome to audit any security vurnerability.

## Prelude

We are born craving ideas and inspiration, and anything that keeps life from becoming hollow and numb. Yet in today’s information age, our inner lives are still strangely primitive: we hunt and gather on the vast, fragmented grasslands of the internet, copying, pasting, and bookmarking one item after another, manually picking out and recording every “worth keeping” spark, like farmers bent over in the fields, harvesting and sorting each crop by hand.

The only small comfort is that our tools for cultivating ideas seem to have improved: writing apps, note-taking tools, and visualization software are everywhere, and AI can generate uncannily realistic text and images at astonishing speed. But at best, this only moves us from slash-and-burn agriculture to refined intensive farming—we still have to go down into the fields ourselves and toil away.

Miorin wants to take one more step: to truly modernize this landscape.
We hope your encounters with the surrounding flow of information can become gentle and effortless—you no longer have to panic about “What if I didn’t write that down?”, or worry about missing a passing sentence, a deep thought, or even an absurd meme that made you laugh. Your clipboard, your folders, and your stray scraps all get quietly caught and turned into reusable raw material.

We also hope that extracting knowledge will no longer rely entirely on manual work: messy, unprocessed information should be automatically tagged, clustered, organized, and archived; fuzzy search should understand your context and intention better, instead of being constrained by a few clumsy keywords you happened to type in the past.

More importantly, you shouldn’t have to fight alone when it comes to connecting and expanding ideas. With the help of an assistant built on the accumulated experience—and biases—of humanity, Miorin sits by your side, observing this raw material together with you, carefully suggesting possible links, common derivative viewpoints, and hidden paths. The real decisions and acts of creation still belong to you—you can follow those paths, or happily overturn them, and in the ongoing process of confirming or challenging what you thought you knew, grow a worldview that is entirely your own.

It’s worth making one thing clear: Miorin is not here to “think” on your behalf, nor to outsource your inner life to a black-box algorithm. What it wants to do is simply to quietly shoulder the heavy, repetitive mechanical work—collecting, organizing, archiving, and retrieving—so that you can put your limited attention back onto what truly matters: understanding, connecting, questioning, and creating.

In the ideal scenario, Miorin is just a quiet little program tucked away in a corner of your computer. It silently catches everything you copy and every file you drop into a watched folder, then weaves them into threads over time, topics, and meaning. When you’re ready to write a diary entry, a note, or even just stare out of the window, it lays these raw materials out like a box of LEGO bricks, occasionally handing you one or two combinations you might enjoy.

This project is still far from “finished”; it’s more like a rough stone that’s in the middle of being carved. Everyone who uses it, complains about it, or modifies it will leave a mark on its surface. We don’t know what a truly modern mental tool for the information age should ultimately look like, but at least we can start here and take a few tentative steps in that direction.

If you’re tired of diving bare-handed into the flood of information again and again, yet unwilling to give up control over your own thinking, then you’re very welcome to run this little experiment with Miorin. Hand it your first fragment, and see what kind of world it might help you piece back together.


## Development

This project is built on Tauri. To get started, make sure you have the Tauri CLI installed:

```
cargo install tauri-cli
```
